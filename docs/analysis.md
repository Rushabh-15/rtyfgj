_Generated by `windcast report` from `data/derived` (snapshot SHA-256 8423e6674ad9…, f004130b53ee…). Delivery days 2026-01-02 to 2026-02-01 (decisions 1–31 January 2026), 23:00–23:00 UK, 1,488 half-hours. Errors are forecast minus outturn in MW: positive means wind came in below forecast, i.e. a shortfall the desk has to cover._

## Headline

At the default cutoff (09:00 UK on D−1, which selects the 07:30 UTC run every day, 15.5–39 h ahead), the forecast ran **1,749 MW above outturn on average** (MAE 2,038 MW, RMSE 2,502 MW). Wind fell short of the forecast in 85% of half-hours; the median shortfall was 1,592 MW and the P10–P90 range -540 to 4,128 MW (4,667 MW wide).

**Buffer.** Buying **4,233 MW** beyond the forecast-implied volume covered wind shortfalls in 90% of half-hours on the fit days (2026-01-02 to 2026-01-16). Applied unchanged to 2026-01-17 to 2026-02-01, it covered **91.5%** of half-hours (95% day-block bootstrap interval 77.9%–100.0%). Refitted on all 31 days it would be 4,128 MW.

**Skill.** The forecast's MAE is 50% lower than persistence (the last outturn published before the cutoff, held for the day: MAE 4,105 MW).

**Where the error comes from.** The gap grows with the forecast level: +787 MW when the forecast is under 6 GW, +3,382 MW when it is 18 GW or more, where wind fell short in 100% of half-hours. Across all forecast runs it is largest at night (05:00 UK: +2,372 MW) and smallest mid-afternoon (15:00: +1,120 MW). Both patterns are consistent with curtailment of wind at high output and low demand (unverified: the balancing data needed to confirm it is outside the brief).

## Buffer back-test (09:00 cutoff)

Buffer = the target quantile of shortfall (forecast − outturn) over the fit days' half-hours. Coverage = share of half-hours with shortfall ≤ buffer. The interval resamples whole test days 5,000 times, because half-hours within a day are strongly correlated.

| Target | Buffer (MW, fitted 2–16 Jan) | Fit coverage | Test coverage (17 Jan–1 Feb) | 95% interval | Refit on all days (MW) |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- | ---: |
| 50% | 1,294 | 50.0% | 31.9% | 18.2%–47.3% | 1,592 |
| 80% | 3,112 | 80.1% | 79.6% | 62.6%–93.5% | 3,123 |
| 90% | 4,233 | 90.0% | 91.5% | 77.9%–100.0% | 4,128 |
| 95% | 5,403 | 95.0% | 96.9% | 90.6%–100.0% | 5,169 |

The upper targets held out of sample; the 50% buffer did not (31.9%), because the systematic gap was larger in the windier second half of January. A median-level buffer is a bet on the bias staying put; the upper quantiles absorb it.

The minimum is not at zero: MAE is lowest at -90 min and the bias-removed spread at -60 min (1,772 vs 1,790 MW at zero, 1.0% lower). Short-lead runs put it at -30 min (1.4% lower). That fits WINDFOR values describing the hour ending at, or the instant of, their label rather than the hour starting at it, or forecast ramps arriving early. Elexon's own forecast endpoints label the value at startTime H with the settlement period starting at H, and the default alignment follows them; the shifted alignment (`WINDCAST_ALIGNMENT_SHIFT_MINUTES=-30`) lowers MAE from 2,038 to 2,004 MW (1.7%).

## Limitations

- **Curtailment.** FUELHH is metered output after any curtailment. The gap grows with forecast level and at night, consistent with curtailment (unverified). If it is curtailment, the buffer may overstate the desk's need at high wind.
- **Embedded wind.** Both datasets cover wind farms with operational metering visible to NESO; smaller distribution-connected wind is in neither and shows up as lower demand, not as wind.
- **Coverage differences.** WINDFOR ran above NESO's own day-ahead incentive forecast for BM wind units in January (see the NESO check in `docs/validation.md`), so part of the gap may be fleet coverage or model differences rather than weather error.
- **Sample.** One winter month: 15 fit days and 16 test days. Errors are correlated within a day, so the effective sample is closer to 31 days than 1,488 half-hours, and the coverage intervals are wide.
- **Resolution.** WINDFOR is hourly; each value is applied to two half-hours. The lag scan suggests the values fit outturn 30–60 minutes earlier than their label.
